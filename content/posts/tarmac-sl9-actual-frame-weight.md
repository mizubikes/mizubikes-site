Specialized lists the S-Works Tarmac SL9 frame at 687 grams. The product page puts that number right at the top and says nothing about what it includes. So I put a Ready To Paint frame on my scale, first exactly as it came out of the box, then with every piece of hardware removed.

The short version: out of the box, my frame weighed 739 g. Fully stripped, it weighed 694 g. That's within about one percent of the claim, but only once nothing is left on it that you'd need to actually ride it.

## The frame

This is a size 54 in the RTP raw carbon finish, which is the lightest finish Specialized sells. The 687 g claim is for a 56, so if anything this frame had an advantage.

Before weighing anything, I checked the scale against a known reference weight.

| Configuration | Measured weight | vs 687 g claim |
|---|---|---|
| Out of the box (UDH hanger, FD hanger, bottle bolts, steering stop chip) | 739 g | +52 g |
| UDH hanger removed | 715 g | +28 g |
| FD hanger and bolts removed | 703 g | +16 g |
| Bottle cage bolts removed | 699 g | +12 g |
| **Bare frame, all hardware removed** | **694 g** | **+7 g** |

## The hardware

| Part | Weight |
|---|---|
| UDH hanger | 24 g |
| Front derailleur hanger and bolts | 12 g |
| Bottle cage bolts | 4 g |
| Steering stop chip | ~1 g |
| **All hardware, weighed together** | **44 g** |

The individual parts add up to about 41 g, but weighed together they read 44 g. That gap comes from the scale rounding each small part to the nearest gram, so 44 g is the number I trust. It also lines up: 739 g minus 44 g is 695 g, within a gram of the stripped frame.

## The fork

| Part | Weight |
|---|---|
| S-Works FACT 12r fork, uncut steerer | 381 g |
| Frame + fork, bare | 1,075 g |
| Frame + fork, with frame hardware | 1,120 g |

Specialized doesn't publish a separate fork weight, so there's nothing to compare against here. Once the steerer is cut to length it will lose a few more grams, so 381 g is the heaviest it will ever be.

## Is 687 g a realistic number?

For the bare frame, yes. Coming in 7 g over the claim, on a frame one size smaller than the one Specialized quotes, is a good result for a production frame.

The problem is what that number describes. To get close to it, I had to remove every hanger and bolt, on the lightest finish Specialized offers. Some riders do run the raw carbon, but most people will choose a painted SL9, and every one of them needs that hardware. Built up the way you'd actually ride it, expect a frame closer to 739 g, and a painted frame will be heavier than that.

For comparison, a painted Tarmac SL8 frame I weighed came in at 815 g, against a claimed 685 g for the S-Works SL8. Paint and hardware add up quickly.

Specialized isn't alone here. There's no industry standard for how frame weights are measured, so most brands quote the lightest number they can. But when you spend almost six thousand dollars on a frameset, the listed weight should reflect what you'll actually build.
