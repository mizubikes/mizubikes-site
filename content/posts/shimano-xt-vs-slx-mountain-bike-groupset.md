**Quick answer:** Shimano XT vs SLX isn't a straight fight anymore. Shimano retired the SLX name in 2026 and moved its slot into Deore M7200. If you want the best mechanical Shimano drivetrain, buy XT M8200. If you want most of the shifting for roughly $230 less, buy Deore M7200 parts.

## What happened to SLX

In mid-June 2026, Shimano launched new mechanical Deore M7200 and M6200 components. A few days later it launched mechanical XT M8200 parts. Reviewers say the new Deore M7200 effectively replaces SLX. Flow Mountain Bike put it bluntly: there's no longer a complete SLX group, though you can still get that level of performance from the M7200 cassette and derailleur.

The naming is confusing. BikeRadar points out that Deore M7200 isn't SLX, even though M7xxx used to mean SLX. The M7200 derailleur and cassette carry that number. The Deore crank, shifter and cheaper derailleur are M6200.

So when someone asks about XT vs SLX today, there are two realistic answers. You can buy XT M8200 or Deore M7200 new. Or you can hunt for leftover SLX M7100 stock at a discount. All three are 12-speed Hyperglide+ on a Micro Spline freehub, so they mix freely. Bikebug notes that the new 12-speed derailleurs work with any combination of Shimano 12-speed shifter, cassette and chain.

## Shimano XT vs SLX in 2026: what you're actually choosing between

Here's how the parts line up. XT and Deore M7200/M6200 prices are US MSRPs from launch coverage, except the XT cassette and crank. The cassette is a US retailer listing, and the crank is the carryover price from the M8100 generation. The SLX column is the old M7100 MSRP.

| Part | XT M8200 | Deore M7200/M6200 | Old SLX M7100 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rear derailleur | $170, 370g claimed | $100, 430g | $95 |
| Shifter | $85 | $30, 129g | $48 |
| 10-51t cassette | $195 | $180 (M7200), 504-510g | $130, about 534g |
| Crank | $190 | $100 (M6200), 735g | $130 |
| Those four parts | about $640 | about $410 | $403 |

The takeaway is that Deore M7200 costs about what SLX did, but the new parts are pricier in places. The M7200 cassette is $50 more than the old SLX one. The XT premium is real but not huge. It's about $230 across those four parts.

## The derailleur: tougher, but not lighter

Both XT M8200 and Deore M7200 use Shimano's new Shadow ES design. It replaces the old friction clutch with a dual-spring chain stabilizer in a slim, wedge-shaped body that's meant to shrug off rock strikes. Shimano says the spring force is up 70% over the old design.

XT gets an alloy cage and solid pulleys. Deore M7200 gets a steel cage, which is part of why it weighs 430g against XT's claimed 370g. XT is also available in a mid-cage version for a 9-45t cassette, which gives more ground clearance. Deore M7200 only comes in long cage for 10-51t.

The XT isn't a lightweight part, though. Flow weighed the M8200 derailleur at 360g, about 80g more than the old M8100. Their review also flagged that the clutch-less design can be noisy. Single-spring Shimano XT purists should know the trade: more rock protection, more grams, a bit more chain noise.

## Shifter, cassette and crank: where the money matters

The shifter gap is $55. On features, it has narrowed a lot. The Deore SL-M6200 gets two-way release and up to three shifts per stroke. BikeRadar's review says it feels plasticky in hand, but that the shifting under load is excellent. If you like a nicer lever, the $85 XT shifter is the cheap upgrade. It also fits any 12-speed Shimano setup.

The cassette is the best XT upgrade for the money. XT lists around $195 at retailers. The Deore M7200 is $180 and weighs about 504-510g, so you'd pay roughly $15 for a cassette that should be around 35-40g lighter. That math is based on the earlier M8100 XT cassette at about 470-475g. Pinkbike measured the M8200 as only 7g lighter than that. If you want durability over weight, the all-steel Deore M6200 cassette is $140, with Flow measuring it at 562g.

The crank is the part to skimp on. The Deore M6200 is $100 and Flow weighed it at 718g. That's more than 100g heavier than the XT crank it was compared against. It has a steel chainring, which is a durability plus. If you're not counting grams, the $90 saved buys a lot of chains.

## Brakes

XT M8200 brakes are $265 per brake per Singletracks. Shimano's new Deore M6220 four-piston brakes are $155 front and $160 rear. Shimano didn't release a new SLX brake. If you're building up a bike, that's $200+ more for XT stopping power, and it's a real consideration.

## Which one to buy

Buy XT M8200 if you ride aggressively, want the alloy-cage derailleur and mid-cage option, or are building a bike you'll keep for years. It's also considerably cheaper than SRAM's mechanical option. Vital MTB's table has the XT M8200 set at $640 against $710 for SRAM's 90 Transmission set. If you're weighing that decision, see our [SRAM Eagle 70/90 vs Shimano SLX/XT/XTR comparison](/posts/new-sram-eagle-70-and-90-vs-shimano-slx-xt-xtr-full-drivetrain-comparison-with-weight-and-price/) for earlier weight and price context.

Buy Deore M7200 if you want the SLX-style value pick. It has the same Shadow ES derailleur tech and the same Hyperglide+ shifting, at a lower price. BikeRadar's verdict is that most riders don't need to spend more.

Mix and match if you can. That spends the extra money where you feel it most for the least cost. ## FAQ

**Is Shimano SLX discontinued?**
Shimano hasn't carried the SLX name into its 2026 mechanical lineup. Reviewers say the new Deore M7200 effectively takes its place, and there's no complete SLX group anymore.

**Is Deore M7200 the same as SLX?**
Not exactly. BikeRadar says M7200 isn't SLX, but it sits in the same slot. The M7200 cassette in particular looks like a refined version of the SLX cassette.

**Can I mix XT and Deore parts?**
Yes. Bikebug says the new 12-speed derailleurs work with any 12-speed Shimano shifter, cassette and chain combination. XT M8200 parts are also compatible with older 12-speed Shimano drivetrains.
