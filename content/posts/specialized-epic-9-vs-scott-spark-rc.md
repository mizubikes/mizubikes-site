**Quick answer:** For a pure race bike, pick the Specialized Epic 9. It's lighter, its geometry is more forgiving at the tall end of the size range, and Pinkbike called it the racer's pick in its 2026 XC group test. Pick the Scott Spark RC if you want a plusher, more trail-capable XC bike, or if you want the cheaper entry build.

That's the short version of Specialized Epic 9 vs Scott Spark RC. Both are 120mm front and rear, both use 29-inch wheels, and both are very expensive at the top end. The real differences are weight, how stiff each one feels, how you control the suspension, and what the cheapest version costs.

## Specialized Epic 9 vs Scott Spark RC at a glance

| | Specialized Epic 9 | Scott Spark RC |
|---|---|---|
| Travel | 120mm / 120mm | 120mm / 120mm |
| Head angle | 65.9° | 65.4°–66.4° (adjustable) |
| Reach / stack | 420–505mm / 594–645mm | 430–505mm / 591–613mm |
| Chainstay | 435–442mm | 434mm |
| Claimed frame weight | 1,589 g (size M, with shock and hardware) | 1,427 g (HMX-SL, size M, no shock) |
| Entry build | Epic 9 Comp, $5,699.99, about 25 lb 15.5 oz | Spark RC Comp, $3,999, about 28.2 lb |
| Top-end price | S-Works, $15,250 | Pinkbike's test build, $12,999 |

A caveat on that frame weight row. The two companies don't measure on the same basis. Specialized's figure includes the shock, and Scott's doesn't. Another reviewer, James Huang at n-1, still put the Spark's lightest frame about 150 g above the S-Works Epic 9.

## Weight and stiffness

The Epic 9 is the lighter bike, and Specialized built the whole bike around that. The Epic 9 replaced the Epic 8, the Epic World Cup and the Epic Hardtail, so it's one platform now. Pinkbike's group test lists the size XL S-Works at 22.5 lb against 24.7 lb for the Scott Spark RC. That's a two-pound gap between two very expensive bikes.

The gap is bigger lower in the lineup. The Epic 9 Comp is listed at about 25 lb 15.5 oz in a medium, while 99spokes lists the Spark RC Comp at 28.2 lb. That's partly a spec difference, because the Spark Comp has a Fox Rhythm 32 fork and aluminum wheels. It's still a real difference on the climbs.

The Epic's stiffness comes with a catch. Pinkbike's testers said the frame may be too stiff for some and isn't as traction-rich as others on test. Singletracks found it a bit jittery on steep descents compared with the Epic 8. If you're coming from an Epic 8, my [Epic 8 vs Epic 9 breakdown](/posts/specialized-epic-8-vs-epic-9-should-you-upgrade/) covers what actually changed. I also wrote about [why the Epic 9 has no Evo variant yet](/posts/specialized-epic-9-vs-epic-8-evo/) if you want something looser.

## Suspension and ride feel

The Spark RC takes a different approach. Scott moved the shock into the downtube and uses an inverted flex-pivot layout, and 99spokes' summary of reviews highlights how sensitive the rear end feels. BikeRadar's first ride described the Flight Attendant version as digging in tenaciously on climbs and coping well when things got chunkier. Pinkbike's Spark reviewer said the geometry and the frame flex are what define the climbing feel.

It isn't flawless. Reviewer roundups on 99spokes flag the low stack, a complicated multi-part short link that collects dirt, and an initially overwhelming Flight Attendant setup. The same roundups say the TwinLoc lever is awkward to use and the rear suspension bobs under power when left open. Remember the lower builds use TwinLoc rather than Flight Attendant, so those complaints hit the cheaper bikes harder.

In Pinkbike's timed segments, the Epic 9 beat the Spark RC on both the climb (2:26 vs 2:31) and the descent (0:50 vs 0:52). That's one test, one tester, and one trail, so treat it as a hint rather than a verdict. It does match what the weights and geometry suggest.

## Geometry and fit

The numbers are close, but the stack heights split by frame size. The Spark's range tops out at 613mm, and 99spokes calls the position aggressive and hands-heavy. The Epic 9 goes up to 645mm in XL. A Pinkbike commenter who's 6'3" said 611mm wasn't going to work for him. That's anecdotal, but it's a fair warning for tall riders with tight hamstrings.

The Spark does have some adjustability. Per Vital MTB's spec sheet, the head angle moves by ±0.6° through rotating headset cups. It also has in-frame storage and room for 2.6-inch tires. 99spokes notes that the Spark is limited to 29-inch wheels. If you've been following the big-wheel experiments, see [32-inch XC bikes](/posts/32-inch-xc-bikes-racing-coming/).

## Price: where the Spark RC wins

The Epic 9 gets expensive fast. Retailers list the [Epic 9 Comp](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Specialized+Epic+9+Comp&tag=mizubikes-web-20) at $5,699.99. It comes with a FACT 11m carbon frame, a SID Select fork, alloy wheels, and a wireless SRAM S1000 Eagle Transmission AXS drivetrain. Above that, Pinkbike lists the Expert at $7,500, the Pro at $12,000 and the S-Works at $15,250.

The [Scott Spark RC Comp](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Scott+Spark+RC+Comp&tag=mizubikes-web-20) is listed at $3,999.99 on Vital MTB, with an Eagle 70 Transmission drivetrain per 99spokes. Moving up, Vital lists the World Cup at $8,499.99, and Pinkbike's test bike was $12,999. Prices vary by retailer and model year, and n-1 quoted a US range starting nearer $5,000. Check current listings before you decide.

One more point against the Spark's top end is the spec. 99spokes' roundup says the Spark's top World Cup build uses the lower-tier HMF frame, because Scott reserves the lightest HMX-SL frame for the SL model.

## Which should you buy?

Buy the Epic 9 if you race XC, climb a lot, or want the lightest bike your budget allows. It's the more single-minded bike, and the reviews suggest it gives up some traction and comfort to be that way.

Buy the Spark RC if your "XC" riding is really fast trail riding, or you're on a tighter budget. The Comp undercuts the Epic 9 Comp by about $1,700, and the suspension is reportedly more supple. Just budget for a heavier bike and check the cockpit and stack height before you commit.

If you're deciding on a hunch, find a shop that stocks both and ride them back to back on the same loop. The difference in how they feel will tell you more than any spec table.

## FAQ

**Is the Epic 9 lighter than the Spark RC?**
Yes. Specialized claims a 1,589 g frame in size M including the shock. On Pinkbike's scale, the S-Works Epic 9 was 22.5 lb against 24.7 lb for the Spark RC.

**Can the Scott Spark RC fit 2.6-inch tires?**
Yes. 99spokes lists clearance for 2.6-inch tires, up from 2.4 inches on the previous generation. The Epic 9 Comp ships with 2.35-inch tires.

**What's the cheapest Epic 9 or Spark RC?**
The cheapest Spark RC I found is the Comp at $3,999. The Epic 9 Comp at $5,699.99 is the cheapest Epic 9 I found listed at US retailers.
