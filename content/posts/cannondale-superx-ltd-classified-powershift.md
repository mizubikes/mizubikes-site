Cannondale just released a new top build of its SuperX gravel race bike, and the interesting part isn't the frame. The SuperX LTD Classified runs a single chainring up front with a Classified Powershift two-speed hub in the back wheel, so you get the range of a 2x drivetrain with no front derailleur.

That speaks directly to the problem I wrote about in [the 1x gearing problem](/posts/gravel-bike-for-road-1x-gearing-problem/): a single chainring makes you choose between a low climbing gear and a fast top end. Classified's answer is to put the second chainring inside the hub.

**Quick answer:** The SuperX LTD Classified is $6,999 in the US, with Shimano GRX 825 Di2, a 46T chainring, an 11-34 cassette and a Classified Powershift hub laced into Reserve 53|48 carbon wheels. The hub shifts in a split second, even under load, and gives you about 451% of gear range with road-style gaps between gears. The tradeoffs are extra weight compared with a premium 2x, a proprietary hub, cassette and thru-axle, a battery to charge, and a small efficiency loss in the low range.

## How the Classified Powershift hub works

The Powershift is a two-speed planetary gear system inside the rear hub. In high, it's a direct 1:1 drive, like any normal hub. In low, it reduces your gearing to about 0.686 of the high range. That works out to a 46T chainring behaving like a 32T, the same jump as a 2x crank.

![Classified Powershift hub on the Cannondale SuperX LTD rear wheel](/images/classified-powershift-hub-superx.webp)
*The Classified Powershift hub. Photo: Cannondale*

The hub itself is mechanical. The shifting signal is wireless: a receiver in Classified's thru-axle handle switches between the two ranges. On most bikes you add a separate Classified button to the bar, and reviewers have called that the weak point. On the SuperX LTD, the hub works straight from the Shimano GRX Di2 shifters, so there's no extra button. That's the most important detail of this build.

## The gearing math

Here's what the SuperX LTD's gearing gives you, as speed at 90 rpm on a 40mm tire, next to the setups from my 1x gearing article:

| Setup | Low gear | Top gear | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| SuperX LTD, 46T + 11-34, Powershift | 6.9 mph | 30.9 mph | 451% |
| Same bike, high range only | 10.0 mph | 30.9 mph | 309% |
| SRAM 1x13, 40T + 10-46 | 6.4 mph | 29.5 mph | 460% |
| Shimano GRX 2x12, 48/31 + 11-36 | 6.4 mph | 32.2 mph | 507% |

The total range is about the same as SRAM's 13-speed XPLR. The difference is how it's spread. SRAM gets its range with a 10-46 cassette that has big jumps at the bottom (32, 38, 46). The Classified setup uses a close-ratio 11-34 cassette twice, so the steps between gears stay small across the whole range, the way a 2x does.

The top gear on the stock 46T ring is a gravel top gear, not a road one. If you want the SuperX to double as a road bike, you could fit a bigger ring. With a 52T, the top gear goes to about 34.9 mph at 90 rpm, the same as a road 52x11, and the low gear is still about 7.7 mph. That's the one-bike setup a normal 1x can't give you.

## Advantages of the Classified hub

**2x range with a 1x front end.** No front derailleur, no second ring, and nothing to clog in mud. Chain retention is better too, since the chain never moves across rings.

**It shifts under load.** You can drop into the low range mid-climb while pushing hard, which a front derailleur hates. Reviewers describe the shifts as near instant and very reliable.

**Tight gear steps.** You get the small jumps of a road cassette instead of the big gaps on a wide 1x cassette. On a fast group ride or a long gravel race, that means you can stay closer to your ideal cadence.

**Big range in the hub, not the cassette.** An 11-34 cassette is lighter and more compact than a 10-46 or 10-52, and the derailleur doesn't have to wrap a giant cog.

**Integrated with Di2 on this bike.** On most Classified setups you get a separate button. Here it's part of the GRX shifters, which takes away the most common complaint.

## Disadvantages of the Classified hub

**Weight.** Reviewers found the system heavier than a premium 2x drivetrain. Classified doesn't hide this, and it's the price of carrying a gearbox in the wheel.

**Proprietary parts.** You need Classified's hub shell, Classified's cassette and Classified's thru-axle. You can't just swap in any wheel. A second wheelset needs its own Classified hub shell, though the internal unit can move between compatible wheels.

**Another battery to charge.** The thru-axle receiver has a rechargeable battery that lasts about 3 to 6 months of riding, and the Di2 system has its own. It's not much, but it's one more thing to forget before a race.

**A small efficiency loss in low.** In the low range the power goes through the planetary gears, which costs a little efficiency. In high it's direct drive. That loss happens only in the gears you use on steep climbs, and nobody has published an independent measurement of how much it is.

**Service.** The axle, bearings, planetary gears and freehub are one sealed unit, and Classified says it's maintenance-free. You can't service the bearings yourself. If something goes wrong, it goes back to Classified.

**Cost.** Classified isn't cheap aftermarket, which is why getting it as part of a complete bike at $6,999 is one of the better ways to try it.

## Who should buy the SuperX LTD Classified?

It makes the most sense if you race gravel or cyclocross, ride in mud, or want one bike that covers gravel and fast road riding without a front derailleur. If you've been on the fence between 1x simplicity and 2x gearing, this is the best of both, as long as you're fine with the weight and the proprietary hub.

If you mostly ride smooth gravel and don't care about road speed, a normal 1x like SRAM's 13-speed XPLR is lighter, simpler and has more wheel options. And if you want 2x without anything proprietary, a gravel bike that takes a front derailleur, like the [Diverge 4](/posts/specialized-crux-5-vs-diverge-4/), still does the job.

For the full breakdown of why 1x struggles on the road, see [the 1x gearing problem](/posts/gravel-bike-for-road-1x-gearing-problem/). For SRAM vs Shimano on gravel, see [SRAM 13-speed vs GRX 12-speed](/posts/sram-13-speed-vs-shimano-grx-12-speed-gravel-2026/).

## FAQ

**How much is the Cannondale SuperX LTD Classified?** $6,999 in the US and €6,999 in Europe. It isn't sold in the UK.

**What does the Classified Powershift hub do?** It's a two-speed hub inside the rear wheel. Low gear reduces your gearing by about 31%, so a 46T chainring acts like a 32T. It gives you a 2x range without a front derailleur.

**Is the Classified hub heavier than a normal drivetrain?** Yes. Reviewers found it heavier than a premium 2x setup, though it saves the front derailleur and second chainring.

**Does the Classified hub need charging?** The thru-axle receiver has a rechargeable battery that lasts about 3 to 6 months of riding. On the SuperX LTD, shifting comes from the GRX Di2 shifters.
