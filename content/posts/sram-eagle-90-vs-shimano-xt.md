**Quick answer:** For most riders on a normal derailleur-hanger frame, Shimano's new XT M8200 mechanical is the better buy. It's cheaper, lighter, and reviewers say it has the nicer shifter feel. Pick SRAM Eagle 90 Transmission if your frame is UDH and you want a tougher, rebuildable derailleur that shifts well under load.

This comparison changed in June 2026. Shimano launched the XT M8200 groupset with a Di2 derailleur and shifter, then added a cable-actuated version for 2026. So the old "Eagle 90 vs XT M8100" arguments are out of date. The right matchup now is Eagle 90 against the XT M8200 mechanical.

If you want the older, broader price-and-weight picture across SLX, XT and XTR, I covered it in [SRAM Eagle 70/90 vs Shimano SLX/XT/XTR](/posts/new-sram-eagle-70-and-90-vs-shimano-slx-xt-xtr-full-drivetrain-comparison-with-weight-and-price/).

## SRAM Eagle 90 vs Shimano XT at a glance

These are the numbers I could verify. Prices are US list or US retailer prices. Weights come from reviewers' scales unless noted.

| | Shimano XT M8200 (mechanical) | SRAM Eagle 90 Transmission |
|---|---|---|
| Derailleur price | $170 | $210 |
| Shifter price | $85 | $55 |
| Cassette price | $195 (10-51T or 9-45T) | $275 (10-52T) |
| Derailleur weight | about 360 g (GS cage) | about 390 g |
| Shifter weight | about 131 g with cable | about 135 g |
| Complete group price | Buy parts separately | $735 |
| Frame requirement | Any derailleur hanger | UDH frame only |
| Rebuildable derailleur | Not a selling point | Yes, SRAM's headline feature |

The sources for those numbers: Shimano's derailleur lists at $170 and the shifters all come in at $85. REI lists the XT M8200 cassette at $195. On the SRAM side, the Eagle 90 derailleur is $210, the shifter $55, the cassette $275, and the complete groupset $735.

Those SRAM prices have crept up. At launch the whole groupset, cranks included, was $670. If you're reading an older review, add roughly $65.

## Price: the gap is mostly the cassette

The derailleur and shifter pair is nearly a wash. XT is $255 and Eagle 90 is $265. The real money is in the cassette, where SRAM's 10-52T costs about $80 more than Shimano's XT cassette. SRAM also sells Eagle 90 as a single $735 kit, which is convenient for a full build or an upgrade.

Shimano's mechanical release only covered the derailleur and shifter. The crank, cassette and brakes were updated with last year's Di2 launch, and the chain carried over. You'll assemble an XT drivetrain from parts. That's easy to do, and it lets you reuse what you already have. The new parts are backward compatible with existing 12-speed mechanical groupsets.

If you only need a new derailleur and shifter, [a Shimano XT M8200 rear derailleur](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Shimano+Deore+XT+M8200+rear+derailleur&tag=mizubikes-web-20) is the cheap way to refresh a tired XT setup.

## Weight: Shimano wins, mostly at the crank

Both derailleurs are heavier than their predecessors. Even so, the XT mech undercuts Eagle 90 by about 30 g. Freehub measured the XT derailleur at 360 g, its 9-45T cassette at 426 g, and the shifter with cable at 131 g. Pinkbike weighed the Eagle 90 derailleur at 395 g.

The bigger gap is the crank. Flow listed the XT M8200 crank at 609 g with 170 mm arms and a 32T ring, no bottom bracket. The Eagle 90 crank is 825 g. That's a 165 mm crank, so the real gap is a bit wider on equal arm lengths. SRAM's complete Eagle 90 group totals 2,085 g. I don't have a verified total for the M8200 build. In my earlier post, XT M8100 was 1,743 g, and the M8200 derailleur is heavier than the M8100's. Expect Shimano to stay a few hundred grams lighter overall.

## Shifting feel and durability

This is where the two split.

Shimano's pitch is feel. The new XT shifter has a shorter lever stroke, crisper actuation and a firmer secondary click. Freehub's reviewer liked it, saying it's a lighter, cleaner, "better" feeling sensation than Eagle 90. BikeRadar's Eagle 90 review points the same way, noting the SRAM shifter doesn't feel as solid as Shimano's.

SRAM's pitch is toughness. Eagle 90 is a Transmission derailleur, so it bolts directly to the frame instead of hanging off a sacrificial hanger. SRAM says it has replaceable skid plates, links and cage, and calls it the most rebuildable derailleur in the Transmission family. Eagle 90's own chain and cassette are the same ones used on the electronic GX Transmission, which is a big part of why it shifts so cleanly under load.

Flow's head-to-head tilts toward SRAM on durability. They flagged that the XT's clutch-less derailleur can be noisy and its shifting isn't as bulletproof as Eagle 90. That fits Shimano's design change. The new derailleurs use a spring-based Shadow ES stabilizer instead of the one-way clutch used on recent mechanical generations. I haven't ridden the M8200, so treat that as what the reporting suggests, not a verdict.

## Frame compatibility decides a lot of purchases

Eagle 90 only works on UDH frames. Its Full Mount design needs a Universal Derailleur Hanger frame. If your bike isn't UDH, the decision is already made. XT M8200 fits any derailleur hanger.

If you're not sure what your frame has, check before you shop. SRAM's lineup shuffle also matters here, since S-Series and Transmission parts generally need to be kept separate. I walked through that in [SRAM Eagle S-Series: One Lineup Replaces Nine](/posts/sram-s-series-eagle-groupsets/).

## Which one should you buy?

Buy Shimano XT M8200 if your bike has a standard hanger, you want the lightest option, or you care most about shifter feel. It's also the better value, especially if you already own a compatible cassette, crank and chain. It's the natural upgrade from the SLX path I covered in [Shimano XT vs SLX](/posts/shimano-xt-vs-slx-mountain-bike-groupset/).

Buy SRAM Eagle 90 if your frame is UDH and you ride hard, ride an e-bike, or want a derailleur you can rebuild instead of replace. If that's you, [an Eagle 90 Transmission groupset](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=SRAM+Eagle+90+Transmission+groupset&tag=mizubikes-web-20) is the more durable mechanical option. You pay extra and carry extra grams for that.

If you're a weight-focused rider who doesn't beat up derailleurs, XT is the easier call. Eagle 90's strengths show up mainly when the trail is hurting your drivetrain.

## FAQ

**Does SRAM Eagle 90 work on a frame without UDH?**
No. The derailleur uses a frame-clamping Full Mount system that only works with UDH frames.

**Is the new Shimano XT M8200 mechanical or Di2?**
Both exist. The mechanical derailleur and shifter join the M8200 groupset alongside the wireless option. The mechanical parts are the ones that compete with Eagle 90.

**Is Eagle 90 still $670?**
Not at SRAM's US site. The complete groupset is listed at $735, though the launch price was $670.
