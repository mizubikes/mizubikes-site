RockShox Flight Attendant reads the terrain, your pedaling and the gear you're in, then switches your fork and shock between open, pedal and lock on its own, faster than you could reach for a lever. It's genuinely good on an XC bike. For a long time it was only on a couple of very expensive bikes. That changed with the 2027 Scott Spark RC, which puts Flight Attendant on four builds, starting at $8,500.

**Quick answer:** The cheapest XC bike with factory Flight Attendant is the Scott Spark RC Team Issue at $8,500. The best value is the Spark RC World Cup at $10,000, which gets you SRAM X0 for about $2,000 less than the Specialized Epic 9 Pro. Pick the Pivot Mach 4 SL Team XX Flight Attendant ($13,899) if descending matters most to you. A used S-Works Epic 8, which came with Flight Attendant as standard, is the budget option.

## How Flight Attendant works on an XC bike

![RockShox Flight Attendant XC system: SID fork control module, SIDLuxe shock motor module, AXS rear derailleur, pedaling sensor or power meter, and Reverb AXS dropper](/images/rockshox-flight-attendant-xc-system.webp)
*The XC Flight Attendant system. Image: RockShox*

The XC version uses a SID or SID SL fork and a SIDLuxe shock, each with its own sensor module and AXS battery. It switches between three modes, Open, Pedal and Lock. It can also run a split state, with the shock firm and the fork open, which is great for seated climbing on rough ground. You can bias it from -2 (more open) to +2 (firmer), and RockShox claims 20 to 30 hours of riding per charge. If the batteries die, it fails to Open, so you're never stuck locked out.

It works best with an AXS power meter, which lets the system learn your effort levels from your last few rides. Without one, it uses a pedal sensor instead. It also uses an AXS derailleur to know what gear you're in.

The tradeoffs are cost and weight. Flow found the XC system adds 166g over a cable-operated setup, or about 81g if you count the power meter you'd probably want anyway. And you're charging two more batteries.

## Scott Spark RC: the best value Flight Attendant bike

![2027 Scott Spark RC SL with RockShox Flight Attendant](/images/scott-spark-rc-sl-2027.webp)
*Scott Spark RC SL. Photo: Scott*

The 2027 Spark RC is the bike that makes Flight Attendant affordable, or at least less unaffordable. Scott puts it on the Team Issue, World Cup, World Cup EVO and SL builds. The frame is new, with Scott's hidden shock tucked inside the frame, 120mm of travel front and rear, and room for a 130mm fork. Scott claims 1,427g for a painted medium frame without the shock. The higher builds also get internal frame storage with Syncros's tube-and-tool kit.

The **World Cup at $10,000** is the one I'd look at first. It has the same tier of SRAM drivetrain as the Epic 9 Pro (X0) at a claimed 10.9 kg, for about $2,000 less. The **Team Issue at $8,500** is the cheapest way to get factory Flight Attendant on any XC bike, with GX AXS and a claimed 11.3 kg. That's heavier, but the suspension is the same system, and you can upgrade the parts as they wear.

The catch with the Spark is the hidden shock. It looks clean, but it makes shock service and setup more of a job than on a bike with an exposed shock. If you do your own maintenance, factor that in.

## Specialized Epic 9 Pro: the lightest frame

The Epic 9 Pro ($11,999.99) is built on the Epic 9 frame, which Specialized says is the lightest full-suspension XC frame it has made (1,589g in medium with the shock). You get 120mm front and rear, a 65.9° head angle and SRAM X0 Transmission.

It is a race bike, though. Pinkbike called it "unapologetically race-focused," and Singletracks found it less confident on descents than the Epic 8 Evo. It's wireless shifting only, cables route through the headset, and the in-frame storage is gone, replaced with an external SWAT box. I went through all of that in [Epic 8 vs Epic 9](/posts/specialized-epic-8-vs-epic-9-should-you-upgrade/). Next to the Spark RC World Cup, the Epic 9 Pro now costs about $2,000 more for a similar spec, so it needs to win on how it rides, and that comes down to what you prefer.

The **S-Works Epic 9** ($15,249.99) gets the same frame and suspension with XX SL and lighter parts. That's $3,250 more than the Pro, and only worth it if you're racing and counting grams.

## Pivot Mach 4 SL Team XX Flight Attendant: best for descending

![Pivot Mach 4 SL Team XX](/images/pivot-mach-4-sl-team-xx.webp)
*Pivot Mach 4 SL Team XX. The standard Team XX shown here uses Fox suspension, and the Flight Attendant version swaps in RockShox. Photo: Pivot*

Pivot redesigned the Mach 4 SL for 2026 around a more trail-capable idea of XC. The head angle is 66°, two degrees slacker than before. The rear travel adjusts between 110mm and 120mm, and it has down tube storage with an exposed, easy-to-service shock.

At $13,899 it's the second-most expensive bike here, but it's also the one built most for riding down as well as up. If your XC riding looks like mine, technical Southern California trails where the descents decide how much fun you have, this is the one I'd lean toward. The standard Team XX with Fox suspension is $11,999 if you decide you don't need Flight Attendant.

## Used S-Works Epic 8: the Flight Attendant bargain

![Specialized S-Works Epic 8 with RockShox SID Flight Attendant fork](/images/s-works-epic-8-flight-attendant.webp)
*S-Works Epic 8. Photo: Specialized*

When RockShox launched XC Flight Attendant in 2024, the S-Works Epic 8 was the only bike that came with it as standard. Now that the Epic 9 has replaced it, more S-Works Epic 8s are showing up on the used market, and prices should keep falling as racers upgrade.

A used S-Works Epic 8 can undercut even the Spark RC Team Issue, and it keeps the things the Epic 9 dropped: in-frame SWAT storage and frame-port cable routing instead of headset routing. Check that both Flight Attendant modules and batteries are included, and ask whether the AXS power meter comes with it, since that's part of what makes the system work well. ## Can you add Flight Attendant to your current bike?

Yes, on some frames. RockShox sells the XC Flight Attendant fork and shock aftermarket, and at launch it listed fitment for bikes including the Canyon Lux World Cup and Lux Trail (2023 and newer), Mondraker F-Podium, Orbea Oiz and Oiz TR (2020-2023), Pivot Mach 4 SL (2023 and newer), Santa Cruz Blur and Blur TR (2022 and newer) and Specialized Epic EVO (2020-2023).

The cost adds up fast. At launch the SID Ultimate Flight Attendant fork was $1,449 and the SIDLuxe Ultimate Flight Attendant shock was $849, about $2,300 before you add an AXS power meter or pedal sensor. If your bike runs Shimano, you'd also want an AXS derailleur for the full system. Now that the Spark RC Team Issue is $8,500 complete, upgrading your old bike rarely makes sense.

That's my situation with my Epic 8 Evo. It runs Fox suspension and a Shimano wireless drivetrain, so going to Flight Attendant would mean replacing the fork, the shock and the derailleur. For me, it's not worth it on that bike.

## Which one I'd buy

For most riders shopping for Flight Attendant, the **Scott Spark RC World Cup** at $10,000 is the pick. It's the best spec for the money, and the Team Issue is there if you need to spend less. If you ride technical trails and care more about the descents, I'd pay the extra for the **Pivot Mach 4 SL**. The **Epic 9 Pro** is the one to choose if you've ridden it and prefer it, or if a lighter frame matters more to you than $2,000.

And if Flight Attendant isn't a must, every bike here also comes in a cheaper non-Flight Attendant version. If you're curious where XC bikes are headed next, [32-inch XC bikes](/posts/32-inch-xc-bikes-racing-coming/) are coming fast, and Scott's own 32-inch Spark prototype is already racing World Cups.

## FAQ

**Which XC bikes come with Flight Attendant?** For 2026-2027, the Scott Spark RC Team Issue, World Cup, World Cup EVO and SL, the Specialized Epic 9 Pro and S-Works Epic 9, and the Pivot Mach 4 SL Team XX Flight Attendant. Yeti offered a Flight Attendant ASR in 2025, and the S-Works Epic 8 came with it from 2024.

**What's the cheapest bike with Flight Attendant?** New, the Scott Spark RC Team Issue at $8,500. Used, an S-Works Epic 8 can be cheaper.

**Is Flight Attendant worth it for XC?** If you race or ride long, varied terrain, yes. Reviewers consistently found it improves pedaling efficiency without you touching a lever. If you mostly ride smooth trails or don't use a lockout much now, it's a lot of money for a small gain.

**How much weight does Flight Attendant add?** About 166g over a cable-operated setup, or about 81g if you count the power meter most riders would add anyway.
