RockShox Flight Attendant reads the terrain, your pedaling and the gear you're in, then switches your fork and shock between open, pedal and lock on its own, faster than you could reach for a lever. It's genuinely good on an XC bike. It's also only offered as a factory option on a handful of expensive bikes. So if you want it, the real question is which bike to get it on.

**Quick answer:** For most riders, the Specialized Epic 9 Pro at $11,999.99 is the XC bike with Flight Attendant to buy. It's the cheapest factory Flight Attendant bike from a major brand, on the lightest XC frame Specialized makes. Pick the Pivot Mach 4 SL Team XX Flight Attendant ($13,899) if you care more about descending and want down tube storage. On a budget, a used S-Works Epic 8 came with Flight Attendant as standard. The S-Works Epic 9 ($15,249.99) only makes sense if you're racing and want XX SL everything.

## How Flight Attendant works on an XC bike

The XC version uses a SID or SID SL fork and a SIDLuxe shock, each with its own sensor module and AXS battery. It switches between three modes, Open, Pedal and Lock. It can also run a split state, with the shock firm and the fork open, which is great for seated climbing on rough ground. You can bias it from -2 (more open) to +2 (firmer), and RockShox claims 20 to 30 hours of riding per charge. If the batteries die, it fails to Open, so you're never stuck locked out.

It works best with an AXS power meter, which lets the system learn your effort levels from your last few rides. Without one, it uses a pedal sensor instead. It also uses an AXS derailleur to know what gear you're in.

The tradeoffs are cost and weight. Flow found the XC system adds 166g over a cable-operated setup, or about 81g if you count the power meter you'd probably want anyway. And you're charging two more batteries.

## Specialized Epic 9 Pro: the best value

The Epic 9 Pro is the cheapest way to get factory Flight Attendant from a major brand, and it's built on the Epic 9 frame, which Specialized says is the lightest full-suspension XC frame it has made (1,589g in medium with the shock). You get 120mm front and rear, a 65.9° head angle and SRAM X0 Transmission.

It is a race bike, though. Pinkbike called it "unapologetically race-focused," and Singletracks found it less confident on descents than the Epic 8 Evo. It's wireless shifting only, cables route through the headset, and the in-frame storage is gone, replaced with an external SWAT box. I went through all of that in [Epic 8 vs Epic 9](/posts/specialized-epic-8-vs-epic-9-should-you-upgrade/).

If you want an XC race bike that pedals as efficiently as possible, this pairing makes sense. Flight Attendant is at its best on a bike that's built to be firm under power and open on descents.

## Pivot Mach 4 SL Team XX Flight Attendant: best for descending

Pivot redesigned the Mach 4 SL for 2026 around a more trail-capable idea of XC. The head angle is 66°, two degrees slacker than before. The rear travel adjusts between 110mm and 120mm, and it now has down tube storage, which the Epic 9 dropped.

At $13,899 it's about $1,900 more than the Epic 9 Pro. Some of that buys a better drivetrain, XX SL instead of X0. The rest buys Pivot's DW-link suspension and a frame that's more willing to be ridden like a small trail bike. If your XC riding looks like mine, technical Southern California trails where the descents decide how much fun you have, this is the one I'd lean toward.

## S-Works Epic 9: only if you're racing

The S-Works gets the same frame and Flight Attendant suspension as the Pro, with SRAM XX SL Transmission and lighter parts throughout. That's $3,250 more than the Pro for the same suspension system and frame shape. Unless you're racing and counting grams, the Pro is the smarter buy, and you can upgrade parts as they wear.

## Used S-Works Epic 8: the Flight Attendant bargain

When RockShox launched XC Flight Attendant in 2024, the S-Works Epic 8 was the only bike that came with it as standard. Now that the Epic 9 has replaced it, more S-Works Epic 8s are showing up on the used market, and prices should keep falling as racers upgrade.

That makes a used S-Works Epic 8 the cheapest way into a factory Flight Attendant XC bike. It also keeps the things the Epic 9 dropped: in-frame SWAT storage and frame-port cable routing instead of headset routing. Check that both Flight Attendant modules and batteries are included, and ask whether the AXS power meter comes with it, since that's part of what makes the system work well. ## Can you add Flight Attendant to your current bike?

Yes, on some frames. RockShox sells the XC Flight Attendant fork and shock aftermarket, and at launch it listed fitment for bikes including the Canyon Lux World Cup and Lux Trail (2023 and newer), Mondraker F-Podium, Orbea Oiz and Oiz TR (2020-2023), Pivot Mach 4 SL (2023 and newer), Santa Cruz Blur and Blur TR (2022 and newer) and Specialized Epic EVO (2020-2023).

The cost adds up fast. At launch the SID Ultimate Flight Attendant fork was $1,449 and the SIDLuxe Ultimate Flight Attendant shock was $849, about $2,300 before you add an AXS power meter or pedal sensor. If your bike runs Shimano, you'd also want an AXS derailleur for the full system. For a lot of riders, that's most of the way to a new bike, so compare the upgrade against the factory builds above before you commit.

That's my situation with my Epic 8 Evo. It runs Fox suspension and a Shimano wireless drivetrain, so going to Flight Attendant would mean replacing the fork, the shock and the derailleur. For me, it's not worth it on that bike.

## Which one I'd buy

If I were buying an XC bike with Flight Attendant today, it would come down to the Epic 9 Pro and the Mach 4 SL. The Epic 9 Pro is the better deal and the better race bike. The Mach 4 SL is the better bike for how I actually ride, with slacker geometry, adjustable travel and somewhere to put a tube. For most people, I'd say the Epic 9 Pro. For technical trail riding, the Mach 4 SL.

And if Flight Attendant isn't a must, a good XC bike with a simple remote lockout still costs thousands less. Every bike here also comes in a non-Flight Attendant version. If you're curious where XC bikes are headed next, [32-inch XC bikes](/posts/32-inch-xc-bikes-racing-coming/) are coming fast, and some of the prototypes are already racing with Flight Attendant forks.

## FAQ

**Which XC bikes come with Flight Attendant?** For 2026, the Specialized Epic 9 Pro and S-Works Epic 9, and the Pivot Mach 4 SL Team XX Flight Attendant. Yeti offered a Flight Attendant ASR in 2025, and the S-Works Epic 8 came with it from 2024.

**Is Flight Attendant worth it for XC?** If you race or ride long, varied terrain, yes. Reviewers consistently found it improves pedaling efficiency without you touching a lever. If you mostly ride smooth trails or don't use a lockout much now, it's a lot of money for a small gain.

**How much weight does Flight Attendant add?** About 166g over a cable-operated setup, or about 81g if you count the power meter most riders would add anyway.
