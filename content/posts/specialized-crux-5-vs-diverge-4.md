**Quick answer:** In the Specialized Crux 5 vs Diverge 4 matchup, buy the Crux 5 if you pin on a number and care about finish time. Buy the Diverge 4 if your gravel riding involves rough roads, long days and bags. They now clear similar tire sizes, so the real split is geometry, weight and Future Shock.

A year ago this was an easy call, because the Crux topped out at 47mm tires and the Diverge was the wide-tire bike. The Crux 5 changed that, and the old rule of thumb no longer works. Here's what actually separates the two.

## Specialized Crux 5 vs Diverge 4: the tire clearance argument is over

The Crux 5 is the new fifth-generation bike. Tire clearance went from 47 mm on the outgoing version to 55 mm (2.2") front and rear, with the 425 mm chainstay length unchanged. The Diverge 4 is rated lower on paper. It's officially rated for a 50mm tire with 7mm of clearance all round, but it also takes a 2.2in tire with 4mm of clearance.

So both bikes can run a 2.2" XC-style tire. The Diverge just leaves less mud room at that size. If you've read my [Crux 5 vs Grail, Kanzo Fast and Kaius](/posts/crux-5-vs-gravel-race-bikes-2026/) comparison, you know clearance isn't what sets the Crux apart from other race bikes either.

For tire choices at either size, my [gravel tire roundup](/posts/best-gravel-tires-mixed-pavement-dirt-2026/) is a good starting point. Specialized's own [Tracer gravel tires](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Specialized+Tracer+gravel+tire&tag=mizubikes-web-20) are what the Crux 5 launch bike used. BikeRadar's photo showed a 50mm Tracer TLR on Roval Terra Aero CL rims.

## Where the Crux 5 is faster

The Crux 5 is the more aggressive of the two. Specialized claims it's 15.2 watts more efficient than the Crux 4 at 45 kilometers per hour. That's a comparison against the old Crux, not the Diverge, so treat it as the direction of travel rather than a head-to-head number.

Weight is the clearer gap. The S-Works Crux 5's FACT 12r frame is listed at 789 grams, and the S-Level's FACT 10r frame at 897 grams. Specialized lists a complete S-Works Crux 5 at 7.1 kg, and Velo weighed a full S-Works build with Terra CLX III wheels at 6.9 kg. By comparison, Specialized's US site lists the Diverge 4 Expert at 8.9 kg. A 2027 retailer listing with Terra C III wheels says 9.36 kg. Those bikes sit at very different prices, so don't read the whole gap as frame weight. Even so, the direction is obvious.

Handling follows the same logic. Velo describes the Diverge 4 as long and slack, which can feel sluggish on the road or light gravel, while the Crux keeps a tight, low front end with no suspension. The Crux 5 also got a 0.5-degree shallower head tube angle and a 0.5-degree steeper seat tube angle versus its predecessor, so it's not as twitchy as an old cyclocross-derived race bike.

The tradeoff is features. Flow Mountain Bike notes the Crux 5 has no suspension, no SWAT storage and no fender mounts. That's fine for a race bike. It's a real limitation if you want one bike for everything.

## Where the Diverge 4 earns its weight

The Diverge 4 carries Future Shock 3.0 up front and a SWAT 4.0 downtube storage box. Specialized claims Future Shock 3.0 is up to 53% smoother and 11 watts faster. Those are brand numbers, so I'd weigh them accordingly.

Reviewers are split on the suspension. Velo's take is blunt: the Diverge 4 lives or dies on the Future Shock technology. Bikepacking.com was less convinced, calling it a system that doesn't make a huge difference and introduces more problems than it solves. That's the honest tradeoff: if you ride a lot of washboard and chunk, it probably helps. If you mostly ride smooth hardpack, you're carrying the weight for nothing.

If you do want more travel-feel, Specialized offers an upgrade to Future Shock 3.3 for $450. Geometry also leans toward stability. Head angle is half a degree slacker, the bottom bracket is 5mm lower and the chainstays are 5mm longer than the previous Diverge. Chainstays are 430mm against the Crux's 425mm.

One detail worth knowing: Flow notes the entire Specialized factory team, including Sofia Gomez Villafañe, rode the Diverge 4 at last year's Unbound Gravel. Racing and Diverge aren't mutually exclusive when the course is 200 miles of rough Kansas.

## What each one costs

Crux 5 pricing starts higher. Bikerumor lists S-Works at $14,000, S-Level at $10,500, Expert AXS at $7,000 and Comp at $4,500. Framesets are $5,800 for the S-Works and $3,500 for the Crux 5 10r.

The Diverge 4 goes lower. Retailer pricing for the 2026 bikes runs from $2,099.99 for the Sport Alloy and $4,199.99 for the Comp Carbon up to $5,999.99 for the Expert SRAM Rival XPLR. The alloy and carbon frames share the same geometry and tire clearance, so in practice the difference is weight. That makes the alloy Diverge a good value bike. My [best gravel bikes under $2,000](/posts/best-gravel-bikes-under-2000-2026/) list covers cheaper options if the Diverge Alloy is still too much. Prices may have shifted for the 2027 model year, so check Specialized's US site before buying.

## Which one should you buy?

Pick the **Crux 5** if you race, ride fast group gravel, or want a drop-bar bike that doubles as a road bike with fat tires. It's the one for a rider who wants low weight and an aggressive position, and doesn't need bags or fenders. If you're thinking of it as a road replacement, I wrote about that in [Crux 5 as a Road Bike](/posts/crux-5-quiver-killer-road-bike/).

Pick the **Diverge 4** if you bikepack, ride rough terrain, commute, or want to spend under $4,000. You get storage, compliance and a calmer front end for a few kilos and a more relaxed fit. A [Diverge 4](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Specialized+Diverge+4&tag=mizubikes-web-20) with a seatpack and frame bag is hard to beat for long days out.

If you're stuck in the middle, ask what your worst ride looks like. Smooth, fast and long favors the Crux. Rough, loaded and remote favors the Diverge. I'd rather be slightly overbiked for my hardest ride than underbiked for my typical one.

## FAQ

**Does the Crux 5 clear bigger tires than the Diverge 4?**
Yes, on paper. The Crux 5 is rated for 55mm tires, and the Diverge 4 is rated for 50mm. The Diverge also accepts a 2.2" tire with 4mm of clearance.

**Is the Diverge 4 alloy worth buying over carbon?**
The geometry and tire clearance are identical, according to Cyclist, and the alloy bikes also get Future Shock and downtube storage. The carbon bikes are lighter, and the alloy Sport starts at $2,099.99.

**Does the Crux 5 have suspension or storage?**
No. Flow Mountain Bike says it has no suspension, no SWAT storage and no fender mounts. If you want those, the Diverge 4 is the Specialized to look at.
