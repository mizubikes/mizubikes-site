Specialized now sells two very different carbon gravel bikes in the same price range. The Crux 5 is an aero race bike that looks like a Tarmac with big tires. The Diverge 4 is the do-everything bike, with Future Shock, frame storage and room for a front derailleur. They start within $500 of each other, so which one should you buy?

**Quick answer:** Buy the Crux 5 if you race, ride fast group gravel rides or want the lightest, quickest bike for mostly smooth to medium gravel. Buy the Diverge 4 if you ride rough terrain, long days, bikepacking trips or want one bike that also handles the road well. The Diverge is heavier, but it's the only one of the two that can run 2x, and that matters more than most people think.

## Crux 5 vs Diverge 4 at a glance

| | Crux 5 | Diverge 4 |
|---|---|---|
| Built for | Gravel racing | All-round gravel, adventure, bikepacking |
| Tire clearance | 55mm | 50mm (or 2.2in MTB) |
| Front suspension | None | Future Shock 3, 20mm |
| Frame storage | None | SWAT 4.0 down tube |
| Drivetrain | 1x only (up to 52T) | 1x or 2x |
| Frame weight (56cm) | 789g (S-Works, FACT 12r) | 950g (S-Works, FACT 12r), 1,150g (FACT 9r) |
| Lightest complete | 7.1 kg (S-Works) | 8.0 kg (Pro LTD) |
| US price range | $3,999 to $13,999 | $3,499.99 to $12,500 |

Both have UDH dropouts, so both work with SRAM's Transmission and XPLR 13-speed drivetrains.

## Geometry: race bike vs all-day bike

Here's how the two compare in a size 56:

| Size 56 | Crux 5 | Diverge 4 |
|---|---|---|
| Reach | 400mm | 400mm |
| Stack | 578mm | 610mm |
| Head angle | 71.5° | 71° |
| Chainstay | 425mm | 430mm |
| BB drop | 78mm | 85mm |
| Wheelbase | 1,039mm | 1,060mm |

Reach is identical, so the difference is almost all height and length. The Diverge's stack is 32mm taller, which puts you in a more upright position without a pile of spacers. Add the Future Shock's 20mm of travel and the Diverge has a noticeably higher, more relaxed front end.

The Diverge also sits 7mm lower at the bottom bracket, has 5mm longer chainstays and a 21mm longer wheelbase. That makes it calmer and more stable on loose, rough descents and with bags on the bike. The Crux's shorter wheelbase and higher bottom bracket make it quicker to turn and accelerate, which is what you want in a race pack.

## Tire clearance: Crux 5 wins, but it matters less than you'd think

The Crux 5 clears 55mm tires. The Diverge 4 clears 50mm, or a 2.2in mountain bike tire. Most riders run 40mm to 47mm on either bike, so both have plenty of room. Where the Crux's extra clearance shows up is mud, since a 45mm tire in a 55mm frame leaves a lot of space for it to clear. If you run wide rims, remember that [your tire will measure wider than the label](/posts/zipp-303-xplr-sw-vs-enve-ses-3-4-tire-width-test/). For how both compare with other bikes, see my [gravel bike tire clearance chart](/posts/gravel-bike-tire-clearance-chart-2026/).

## Future Shock: the Diverge's biggest advantage

The Diverge 4 uses Future Shock 3, which gives 20mm of hydraulically damped travel above the head tube. The Pro LTD and Pro get the adjustable 3.3 version. The Expert, Comp and Sport get the non-adjustable 3.2, which uses swappable springs. It doesn't make the Diverge a mountain bike, but on washboard and chunky fire roads it takes a lot of the sting out of your hands and shoulders, especially late in a long day.

The Crux 5 has no suspension at all. Its compliance comes from the frame, seatpost and tire volume. On smooth to medium gravel that's fine, and it's lighter and simpler. On rough terrain for hours, the Diverge will leave you less beat up.

## 1x vs 2x: the difference most people miss

The Crux 5 is 1x only. Specialized dropped front derailleur support to fit 55mm tires with short chainstays. The Diverge 4 ships 1x, but the frame also takes a 2x setup with a wider-chainline crank.

That matters if you want one bike for gravel and road. As I worked out in [the 1x gearing problem](/posts/gravel-bike-for-road-1x-gearing-problem/), a 1x setup gives you either a low gear for steep gravel or a road top end, not both. With 2x on the Diverge, you can have both. On the Crux 5, you pick a chainring for the riding you do most.

## Weight

The Crux is the lighter bike at every level. The S-Works Crux 5 frame is a claimed 789g in a 56, and the complete bike is 7.1 kg. The S-Works Diverge frame is 950g, and the FACT 9r Diverge frame that most of the lineup uses is about 1,150g. The lightest complete Diverge, the Pro LTD, is about 8.0 kg, and the Comp Carbon is just under 10 kg.

Some of the Diverge's extra weight is the Future Shock, the SWAT storage and the 2x support. If you'd carry a frame bag and tools anyway, the real gap is smaller than the numbers suggest.

## Which one should you buy?

**Buy the Crux 5 if:**
- You race gravel or want to.
- Your rides are mostly smooth to medium gravel, fire roads and pavement at a fast pace.
- You care about weight and want a bike that feels quick and sharp.
- You're fine with 1x.

**Buy the Diverge 4 if:**
- You ride rough terrain, long days or multi-day trips.
- You want a more upright position without stacking spacers.
- You want frame storage instead of a bag.
- You want one bike for gravel and road, with the option of 2x.

For most people who ride gravel for fun, the Diverge 4 is the better bike. It's more comfortable, more versatile and cheaper at most build levels. The Crux 5 is the better race bike, and if that's what you want, nothing else in Specialized's lineup comes close.

## FAQ

**Is the Crux 5 or Diverge 4 better for long rides?** The Diverge 4. It has a taller stack, Future Shock front suspension and frame storage, which all help on long days.

**Can the Crux 5 run a 2x drivetrain?** No. The Crux 5 is 1x only. The Diverge 4 can run 1x or 2x.

**Which is lighter, the Crux 5 or Diverge 4?** The Crux 5. The S-Works Crux 5 frame is a claimed 789g in a 56, against 950g for the S-Works Diverge and about 1,150g for the standard carbon Diverge.

**What's the tire clearance on the Crux 5 and Diverge 4?** The Crux 5 clears 55mm tires. The Diverge 4 clears 50mm tires, or a 2.2in mountain bike tire.
