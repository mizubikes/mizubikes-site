Wide gravel rims are everywhere now, and the pitch is always the same: a wider rim supports the tire better and gives it a rounder, more stable shape. That part is true. What gets mentioned less is that a wider rim also makes the tire wider, and on a frame that's already close to its limit, that extra width can be the difference between clearing and rubbing.

So I measured it. Same tire, same bike, two rims with very different internal widths, at two pressures.

## The setup

- **Bike:** 2023 Specialized Crux
- **Tire:** [Specialized Tracer TLR 50c](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Specialized+Tracer+TLR+700x50&tag=mizubikes-web-20) in the rear on both wheels
- **Front:** 2.1" Schwalbe Thunder Burt
- **Rim 1:** ENVE SES 3.4, 25mm internal
- **Rim 2:** [Zipp 303 XPLR SW](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Zipp+303+XPLR+SW&tag=mizubikes-web-20), listed at 32mm internal. I measured it at **32.5mm**.

Both are hookless rims, so tire choice and pressure limits matter. Check each rim maker's approved tire list before you copy this setup.

## The results

| Rim | Internal width | 25 psi | 50 psi |
|---|---|---|---|
| ENVE SES 3.4 | 25mm | 50.4mm | 51.6mm |
| Zipp 303 XPLR SW | 32.5mm (measured) | 52.6mm | 54.1mm |
| **Difference** | **+7.5mm** | **+2.2mm** | **+2.5mm** |

The same 50c tire measured 2.2mm wider at 25 psi and 2.5mm wider at 50 psi on the Zipp. On the ENVE it was already a little over its labeled size, and on the Zipp it measured over 54mm, more than 4mm past what's printed on the sidewall.

Two patterns stand out. First, roughly every 3mm of extra internal rim width added about 1mm of tire width. That's a useful rule of thumb when you're sizing tires for a new wheelset. Second, pressure matters less than rim width but isn't nothing. Going from 25 to 50 psi added 1.2mm on the ENVE and 1.5mm on the Zipp. Clearance you check at low pressure in the garage will shrink a bit at higher pressure.

## What this means for clearance

Tire clearance specs are written around a tire's labeled size on a "normal" rim, and a lot of the current gravel wheels are wider than what those specs assumed. If your frame says it fits a 50mm tire and you put that 50mm tire on a 32mm internal rim, you're really fitting a 53 to 54mm tire. On this Crux the rear was usable but tight. Fine on dry dirt, not something I'd run in mud.

So when you're shopping for wide rims, size your tires to the measured width, not the label. If you're near the limit, go down one tire size when you move to a wider rim. Check the rear triangle and the chainstay yoke first, because that's where frames run out of room.
