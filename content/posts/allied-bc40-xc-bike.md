The Allied BC40 isn't new. It came out in 2022 and got a lighter carbon layup and a new cockpit in its last update. But it's the XC bike I keep going back to look at. I haven't ridden an Allied yet. This isn't a review. It's why this particular bike has stuck with me, and what I'd want to find out if I ever get one under me.

**Quick answer:** The Allied BC40 is a 120mm carbon XC and downcountry bike from Bentonville, Arkansas. It's known for excellent pedaling efficiency, slack-for-XC geometry and a boutique finish with custom paint options. Allied's sale through December 31 has the GX Transmission complete at $5,250 (normally $7,000) and the frameset at $2,950 (normally $4,000).

## It just looks right

Most XC bikes look like XC bikes. Skinny tubes, lots of hardware, a shock bolted wherever it fits. The BC40 has clean lines, a low top tube that sweeps into the seatstays, and a finish that looks hand-built, because it partly is. Allied designs, prototypes and tests the frame in-house in Bentonville. It makes the suspension link, brake mount and stem there too, and paints and assembles the bikes there. The carbon front and rear triangles are molded at a factory in Asia, like almost every carbon frame.

The paint is a big part of the appeal. You can order stock colors, or pick "custom" and work with Allied's design team on your own scheme. Very few brands at this size offer that on a full-suspension carbon bike.

## How it compares to my Epic 8 Evo

My current XC bike is an [Epic 8 Evo](/posts/specialized-epic-9-vs-epic-8-evo/), which I've set up to ride harder trails than an XC bike normally sees. So the first thing I looked at was whether the BC40 would be a step sideways or a step forward.

| Size Large | Allied BC40 | Specialized Epic 8 Evo |
|---|---|---|
| Travel F/R | 120 / 120mm | 130 / 120mm |
| Head angle | 66.5° | 65.4° |
| Reach | 475mm | 470mm |
| Stack | 622mm | 613mm |
| Wheelbase | 1,210mm | 1,214mm |
| Chainstay | 435mm | 435mm |

They're closer than I expected. The BC40 is a degree steeper at the head tube and a little taller at the front, with nearly identical reach, wheelbase and chainstays. On paper, it's a slightly more race-oriented take on the same idea: an XC bike that doesn't fall apart when the trail gets rough. Allied also lets you set it up at 100mm or 110mm travel with a different fork and shock, so you can make it more of a pure race bike.

## What reviewers say

Escape Collective's long-term review is the one I keep coming back to. The headline is pedaling efficiency. The reviewer called it one of the best-pedaling bikes in the downcountry category, with a rear end that stays calm under power and holds momentum almost like a hardtail. The slack front end and very stiff frame made it confident on steep, technical descents.

The weak point was small-bump compliance, which they rated as only so-so. The rear end wasn't as supple on chattery ground as some rivals. They also noted the bike rides shorter than its reach number suggests, and recommended a stem about 10mm longer than you'd normally pick. Some smaller complaints from that review, like a missing chain guide and an overly stiff cockpit, were addressed in the update: the BC40 now has a chain guide and Allied's lighter DUET bar and stem.

That tradeoff, great pedaling but less plush on small bumps, is the thing I'd most want to feel for myself. On the rocky, chattery trails I ride, small-bump compliance matters, and I'd want to know how the BC40 compares to my Epic 8 Evo there.

## Sizing

I'm 5'10", which puts me at the top of Allied's Medium (5'6" to 5'10") and inside the Large (5'9" to 6'0"). Since the bike rides short, I'd go Large. That also matches the roughly 475mm reach I like on an XC bike. If you're between sizes, Escape's advice points the same way: size up, or run a longer stem.

I'd want to ride one first. It's the small-bump question more than anything. But if Allied's sale prices hold, the GX complete or the frameset with a custom paint job is about as good a reason as any to try a boutique bike. ## FAQ

**Is the Allied BC40 made in the USA?** Partly. It's designed, tested, painted and assembled in Bentonville, Arkansas, and Allied makes the suspension link, brake mount and stem there. The carbon front and rear triangles are molded in Asia.

**How much travel does the Allied BC40 have?** 120mm front and rear by default, and it can be set up at 110mm or 100mm.

**How much does the Allied BC40 frame weigh?** Allied's target is about 1,950g for a size Large with the RockShox SIDLuxe shock.
