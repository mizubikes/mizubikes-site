If you're shopping for a new mechanical mountain bike drivetrain, you've likely come across **SRAM Eagle 70 and Eagle 90**. They're SRAM's mechanical versions of its Transmission groupsets, and they bring that hangerless, frame-mounted shifting to a much more accessible price. But how do they compare to Shimano's popular SLX, XT and XTR?

In this post, I break down the major contenders by **weight, MSRP** and overall value: **SRAM SX, GX, X01, Eagle 70 and Eagle 90**, and **Shimano SLX, XT and XTR**.

**Quick answer:** **Shimano SLX M7100** is still the best value: it's lighter than Eagle 70 and costs less. **Eagle 90** is the pick if your frame is UDH and you want Transmission-style shifting without paying for AXS. **XT M8100** is the sweet spot for mechanical weight savings. The only reason to buy **XTR M9100** is if you're chasing grams.

## The Catch With Eagle 70 and 90: Your Frame Has to Be UDH

This is the part spec sheets bury. Eagle 70 and 90 use SRAM's **Full Mount** derailleur, which bolts directly to the frame through a UDH (Universal Derailleur Hanger) instead of hanging off a derailleur hanger. If your frame isn't UDH, they won't fit. They're also not cross-compatible with older mechanical Eagle parts, so you're replacing the derailleur, shifter, cassette, chain and crank together.

There's one useful exception: Eagle 70's XS-1270 cassette is the only T-Type cassette that fits a standard Shimano-style spline freehub. You can upgrade without buying a new XD driver, which helps on older wheels.

Shimano's mechanical groups work with any frame that takes a regular derailleur hanger, which is still most bikes on the used market. If you're not sure what your frame has, I wrote about [checking the dropout before you commit to SRAM](/posts/udh-sram-1x13-gravel-frame-compatibility/).

## Key Takeaways

### Best Value Drivetrain

The **Shimano SLX M7100** stands out for riders who want modern 12-speed shifting, solid weight savings and reliable performance at a low cost. It's about 430g lighter than Eagle 70 for about $100 less, and it fits any frame.

### New Budget Options From SRAM

**SRAM Eagle 70** and **Eagle 90** are strong entries in the mechanical drivetrain market.

- Eagle 70 is heavier than SX, but it gets you Transmission's hangerless shifting and durability for a modest price increase.
- Eagle 90 is lighter and more refined, which makes it a good mid-tier choice for trail and XC builds on UDH frames.

### Premium Performance

For lightweight builds or high-performance riding:

- **SRAM X01 Eagle** has a refined feel and is much lighter than GX or Eagle 90. It's a previous-generation group now, so look for it on sale.
- **Shimano XTR M9100** is the lightest option and the most expensive, so it's best for serious racers chasing every gram.

## 2026 Update: Shimano Goes Wireless

Since this post first went up, Shimano launched wireless Di2 for mountain bikes. **XTR Di2 M9200** arrived in mid-2025, followed by **XT Di2 M8200** and **Deore Di2 M6200** that fall. Mechanical XT M8100 and SLX M7100 are still the cable options. Here are the key Di2 parts:

| Shimano Di2 part | XTR Di2 (M9200) | XT Di2 (M8200) | Deore Di2 (M6200) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rear derailleur | 390 g, $665 | 450 g, $670 | 497 g, $435 |
| Shifter | 98 g, $220 | 97 g, $190 | 85 g, $140 |
| Cassette | 9-45t, 329 g, $500 | 10-51t, 464 g, $195 | Carried over |

*Source: [BikeRadar real-world weights](https://www.bikeradar.com/features/tech/shimano-xtr-xt-and-deore-di2-real-world-weights), October 2025.*

So the real 2026 decision is no longer just SRAM vs Shimano. It's cable vs wireless. If you want wireless, compare Shimano's Di2 groups against SRAM's AXS Transmission, which I broke down in [SRAM Eagle S-Series: One Lineup to Replace Nine Drivetrains](/posts/sram-s-series-eagle-groupsets/). If you want cables, the table above still holds, and SLX is still the value pick.
