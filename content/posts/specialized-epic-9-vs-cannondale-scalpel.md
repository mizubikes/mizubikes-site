**Quick answer:** Pick the Specialized Epic 9 if you're racing and weight, wireless shifting and Flight Attendant matter to you. Pick the Cannondale Scalpel if you want a similar 120mm XC bike that costs less at the bottom of the range and has a slightly steeper, more playful front end. Geometry is closer than the marketing suggests, so budget and spec should decide it.

I haven't ridden either one for this post. This comparison comes from the published specs, US pricing and what the reviewers who rode them reported.

## Specialized Epic 9 vs Cannondale Scalpel on paper

Both are carbon, 29-inch, 120mm front and rear, with a threaded bottom bracket and UDH-style dropouts. The numbers below are for the current US lineup. Cannondale's prices are list prices from launch and its own site, and retailers often discount them.

| | Specialized Epic 9 | Cannondale Scalpel |
|---|---|---|
| Travel | 120mm / 120mm | 120mm / 120mm |
| Head angle | 65.9° | 66.6° |
| Seat angle | 76° | 75.5° |
| Reach range | 420-505mm | 425-510mm |
| Chainstay | 435-442mm | 434-446mm |
| Rear suspension design | Flex pivot | FlexPivot chainstay |
| Entry price (USD) | $4,699.99 (Sport) | $4,000 (Scalpel 4, launch list) |
| Mid price | $5,699.99 (Comp) / $7,499.99 (Expert) | $6,500 (Scalpel 2) |
| Top price | $15,249.99 (S-Works) | $14,000 (LAB71) |
| Auto-suspension option | Flight Attendant on Pro and S-Works | Manual lockout only |

The geometry figures come from [Pinkbike's Epic 9 group test](https://www.pinkbike.com/news/review-specialized-epic-9-2026-xc-group-test.html) and [BikeRadar's Scalpel review](https://www.bikeradar.com/reviews/bikes/mountain-bikes/full-suspension-mountain-bikes/cannondale-scalpel-1-lefty-review). Specialized says the Epic 9 stays close to the Epic 8's geometry, so this is not a new-handling story.

The Epic is half a degree slacker at the head, and the Scalpel's seat angle is a touch shallower. Reach and chainstay lengths overlap almost completely. Cannondale also scales chainstay length with frame size, which Specialized does too. If one of these feels very different from the other, it will come from the suspension, tires and fork, not the frame numbers.

## Weight: the Epic 9's real advantage

Specialized claims the Epic 9 frame weighs 1,589g with shock and hardware, [206g less than the Epic 8](https://www.specialized.com/us/en/epic-9-expert/p/4274033). It also dropped the in-frame storage to get there. Pinkbike notes that internal storage is gone and the rear brake line now runs through the head tube.

Complete bikes show the same trend. [Bikerumor's first ride](https://bikerumor.com/specialized-s-works-epic-9-first-ride-impressions-15k-buys-a-very-fast-light-bike/) put the S-Works Epic 9 at 21.29 lb without pedals. The Epic 9 Expert's claimed weight is 24 lb 2.2 oz, and the Comp is claimed at 25 lb 15.5 oz.

The Scalpel is not heavy. At launch Cannondale's Series 1 frame was quoted at 1,980g and the LAB71 frame at 1,780g, though those figures aren't clearly comparable to Specialized's with-shock number. Stated weight for the LAB71 complete bike was 23 lb at launch. Blister measured a 2024 Scalpel 2 in size large at 25.6 lb. Specialized is ahead on weight, but a size-matched, same-day scale test would be the fair comparison.

## Price: where the Scalpel wins

The Epic 9 range now starts lower than it did at launch. [Singletracks reported](https://www.singletracks.com/mtb-gear/a-26-5lb-specialized-epic-9-mtb-now-costs-less-than-5k-with-launch-of-more-affordable-builds/) the Sport at $4,699.99 and the Comp at $5,699.99. Both skip the SWAT storage and use alloy wheels. The Sport has a RockShox Reba fork and a SRAM S1000 Transmission.

The Scalpel 4 is still about $700 cheaper at its $4,000 launch price, with a Shimano Deore/XT drivetrain, a RockShox SID fork and 2.4-inch tires. Cannondale's US page also showed the Scalpel 1 at $8,499, marked down from $9,500, and the LAB71 at $14,000. Check the current price before you buy. Pinkbike's 2025 Scalpel review noted that Cannondale undercut Specialized by about $1,000 at the entry level, and the gap has narrowed since.

The middle of the range is where it gets interesting. The Scalpel 2 at $6,500 has a SRAM GX Eagle Transmission, SID Select+ suspension and HollowGram carbon wheels. The Epic 9 Expert costs $7,499.99 and adds Roval Control wheels and a lighter claimed weight. The Scalpel 2 looks like the better value on paper, and the Expert is the better race weapon.

At the very top, the S-Works Epic 9 costs $1,250 more than the Scalpel LAB71. Bikerumor called the Epic's price too high, so it's not a bargain either way.

## Suspension control and the fork

This is the clearest difference in how the two bikes work. The Epic 9 Pro and S-Works use RockShox Flight Attendant, which adjusts the suspension automatically. The Epic 9 Expert and below don't have it. If you want it, see my list of [XC bikes with Flight Attendant](/posts/xc-bikes-with-flight-attendant/).

The Scalpel makes you choose the mode yourself. In a [downcountry group test](https://enduro-mtb.com/en/best-downcountry-bike-comparison-test/), Enduro reported that the Cannondale's remote worked without accidental mode changes but needs manual selection between Open, Pedal and Lock.

![Cannondale Scalpel in black with RockShox suspension and a SRAM Transmission drivetrain](/images/cannondale-scalpel-rockshox-build.webp)
*Most Scalpel builds use a conventional RockShox or Fox fork instead of the Lefty.*

Then there's the Lefty. In North America it came only on the LAB71 at launch, and Cannondale's US site still lists the LAB71 with the Lefty Ocho Carbon. The rest of the Scalpel range uses RockShox or Fox. The Lefty is smooth and precise, but it needs specialized service tools and shops that can work on it. Specialized uses standard RockShox forks throughout.

## Which one should you buy

Buy the Epic 9 if you race XC and want the lightest bike, wireless shifting and the option of Flight Attendant. The Expert is the sensible entry point to the real race build, with carbon Roval wheels. The Sport and Comp are good value if you accept alloy wheels.

Buy the Scalpel if your budget is tight and you want carbon wheels and a dropper at a lower price. It also suits you if you like the Lefty or a slightly steeper head angle. Its geometry is nearly the same as the Epic's, and it's less of a weight-first bike.

If you're deciding between the old and new Epic, read [Epic 8 vs Epic 9](/posts/specialized-epic-8-vs-epic-9-should-you-upgrade/). If other brands are in your search, [Epic 9 vs Scott Spark RC](/posts/specialized-epic-9-vs-scott-spark-rc/) covers another rival.

## FAQ

**Is the Epic 9 lighter than the Scalpel?**
Specialized claims the Epic 9 is the lightest full-suspension XC race frame, at 1,589g with shock and hardware. Cannondale's quoted frame figures don't clearly match that basis, so I'd treat the Epic as lighter but not by a proven margin.

**What is the cheapest Epic 9?**
The Epic 9 Sport is $4,699.99 at Specialized US. It has a SRAM S1000 Transmission, RockShox Reba fork and alloy wheels, and a claimed weight of 26 lb 4.1 oz.

**Is a new Cannondale Scalpel coming?**
Some sites report a 2027 Scalpel with a new Lefty fork arriving in late 2026 or early 2027. I found no official confirmation, so treat it as a rumor and check for discounts on the current model.
